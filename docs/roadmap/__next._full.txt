15:T1015,<p>KIRA is v0.1 and honest about it. The extraction and answering path works end to end on real
repositories; the parts that make it safe to put in front of a customer are still ahead.</p>
<h2>v0.1 — working now</h2>
<p><code>init</code>, <code>extract</code>, <code>status</code>, <code>ask</code>. Files only, BM25 only, no infrastructure.</p>
<p>Good enough to run in CI and use from a terminal, <strong>inside your team</strong>. Commit <code>.knowledge/</code>,
re-extract changed areas on merge, and let the people who answer customer questions ask it first.</p>
<h2>v0.2 — the eval loop</h2>
<p>Generated questions per area, plus cross-area questions and deliberately <strong>out-of-scope</strong> ones,
because a system that cannot say &quot;I don&#39;t know&quot; scores well on coverage and fails in production.
Every answer is scored by an independent judge; weak ones become a gap backlog, alongside the real
questions that retrieved nothing at runtime.</p>
<p>Also: the Batch API for full rebuilds at half price, and optional embeddings as a hybrid layer for
large bases.</p>
<div class="callout tint-brand"><div class="callout-label">Important</div><div class="callout-body"><p>This is the gate before anyone outside your team sees an answer. Until the eval loop exists there
is no way to tell &quot;answers well&quot; from &quot;answers confidently&quot; — and the two failures that shipped in
v0.1 both slipped past a full unit-test suite and a type-check. One of them was intermittent.</p>
</div></div><h2>v0.3 — the widget</h2>
<p>An HTTP endpoint over the same answering path, a React component and a script tag, the reader&#39;s
screen and plan passed in as context, working deep links, and one adapter for handing a
conversation to a human.</p>
<p>The endpoint reuses <code>ask</code> exactly. If the CLI answers well, the widget answers well; there is one
code path to fix.</p>
<h2>v0.4 — production shape</h2>
<p>Optional Postgres and pgvector backend, atomic knowledge versions so a deploy is a switch rather
than a race, multi-tenancy, and analytics over the gap backlog.</p>
<h2>Deliberately out of scope</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Not a coding assistant.</strong> Claude Code, Cursor and friends already answer a developer&#39;s
questions about a repository, and an agent with grep beats naive retrieval at that job. KIRA&#39;s
reader cannot open the code.</li>
<li><strong>Not an agent that acts inside your product.</strong> An assistant that clicks things on a customer&#39;s
behalf is a different product with a different risk profile.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Honest limits</h2>
<p><strong>Extraction cost and latency scale with the repository.</strong> Unchanged areas are skipped entirely,
the instruction prefix is cached, and both <code>models</code> and <code>effort</code> are per-stage settings. But a
first full run on a large codebase is a batch job measured in minutes and dollars, not seconds.</p>
<p><strong>Knowledge goes stale.</strong> Every fact records the source hash and commit it came from, <code>kira status</code>
reports the drift, and CI should re-extract changed areas on merge — so drift is visible rather
than silent. Nothing stops you from ignoring it.</p>
<p><strong>Someone has to review what was generated.</strong> That is why knowledge lives in git rather than a
database. The first wrong answer a customer sees costs more trust than the tool earns in a month,
and no amount of prompt engineering substitutes for a person reading the diff before it ships.</p>
<h2>When to put it in front of customers</h2>
<p>Three gates, and they are about who reads the answer rather than whether the code is finished:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>Now (v0.1)</strong> — internal tool. CI job, committed knowledge, your team asking from a terminal.</li>
<li><strong>After v0.2</strong> — show it to people, once the coverage report gives you a number you can defend.</li>
<li><strong>After v0.3</strong> — in the product behind a flag, for internal users and one friendly customer
first, with the knowledge reviewed in a pull request rather than auto-published.</li>
</ol>
