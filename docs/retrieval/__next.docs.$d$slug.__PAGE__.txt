3:Tec4,<p>A knowledge base is hundreds of facts, not millions of documents, and every fact carries a
hand-quality title and summary. So retrieval is BM25 — no embeddings, no vector store, nothing to
run. It costs nothing, works offline, and returns the same result twice, which matters more than
marginal recall when the output is customer-facing.</p>
<h2>The pipeline</h2>
<h3>1. Rewrite</h3>
<p>The question is rewritten into the language the facts are written in
(<code>knowledge.language</code>), and reduced to the words that would appear in the article answering it.</p>
<div class="callout tint-brand"><div class="callout-label">Important</div><div class="callout-body"><p>This step is not an optimisation, it is a correctness fix. Lexical retrieval cannot cross
languages: a Russian question against an English knowledge base scores exactly zero, retrieval
never runs, and the assistant honestly reports ignorance about something it knows perfectly
well. That failure is invisible in production — it looks like a polite gap in coverage rather
than a bug.</p>
</div></div><p>Skip it with <code>--raw-query</code> when you know the question is already in the right language.</p>
<h3>2. Score</h3>
<p>BM25 over <code>title</code> (×3), <code>summary</code> (×2) and body, with a Unicode-aware tokenizer — an ASCII-only
pattern silently drops every Cyrillic, Greek or CJK word.</p>
<h3>3. Boost by place</h3>
<p>If the caller passed <code>--route</code>, facts whose own route matches are boosted:</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Relation</th>
<th>Example against <code>/project/42/domains</code></th>
<th>Boost</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody><tr>
<td><code>exact</code></td>
<td><code>/project/:id/domains</code></td>
<td>×1.4</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>ancestor</code></td>
<td><code>/project/:id</code></td>
<td>×1.2</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><code>descendant</code></td>
<td><code>/project/:id/domains/dns</code></td>
<td>×1.1</td>
</tr>
</tbody></table>
<p>The boost is multiplicative on purpose: it reorders comparable matches instead of lifting an
irrelevant fact above a relevant one. A fact BM25 scored near zero stays near zero.</p>
<h3>4. Gate</h3>
<p>Facts whose <code>requires</code> conflict with the reader&#39;s plan, role or flags are dropped. A gate only
excludes when the caller actually supplied that context — unknown means unfiltered.</p>
<h3>5. Filter by audience</h3>
<p>An <code>operator</code> may read <code>end_user</code> facts; a reader never sees facts written above their level. A
<code>developer</code> fact never reaches an end user.</p>
<h3>6. Quarantine low confidence</h3>
<p>Anything the extractor flagged <code>low</code> stays out unless <code>--include-low</code> is passed.</p>
<h3>7. Diversity</h3>
<p>Candidates are walked best-first and one is kept unless it largely repeats a fact already
selected — word-set overlap above 0.6. Five slots hold five distinct facts rather than one fact
restated five times, which matters because extraction batches can produce overlapping facts about
the same thing.</p>
<h2>Why not embeddings</h2>
<p>Three reasons, in order of weight:</p>
<ol>
<li><strong>First-run experience.</strong> Anthropic ships no embeddings endpoint, so vectors mean a second
provider and a second key before anyone sees a single answer.</li>
<li><strong>Determinism.</strong> The same question returns the same facts. When the output is shown to a
customer, being able to reproduce a bad answer is worth more than a slightly better one.</li>
<li><strong>Scale.</strong> BM25 is strong at hundreds-of-documents scale with good titles. It starts to hurt
much later than people expect.</li>
</ol>
<p>Optional embeddings arrive in v0.2 as a hybrid layer for large bases — exactly where BM25 starts
to hurt, and not before.</p>
